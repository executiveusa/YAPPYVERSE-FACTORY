# YAPPYVERSE — Narrator & Mystery Canon

## Status
LOCKED SHARED STORY RULE

## Core premise
The Yappyverse is told through incomplete witnesses, surveillance systems, recovered evidence, rumors, and characters with limited or compromised knowledge. Nobody gets to explain the whole truth at once.

Pauli is the central protagonist, but his identity, motives, origin, and mission are deliberately withheld. His influence should be visible before he is.

## Narrator
A recurring unidentified storyteller narrates portions of the series.

### Voice
- delivered through ElevenLabs;
- conversational crime-story energy rather than formal documentary narration;
- dry, observant, lived-in, Pacific Northwest compatible;
- intelligent without sounding polished or omniscient;
- short interventions only;
- never narrates what the image already shows.

### Identity rule
The narrator is a character, not a neutral voice-of-God.

The audience initially does not know:
- who the narrator is;
- whether the narrator is speaking from the present, the future, or another point in the timeline;
- exactly how the narrator knows Pauli;
- whether the narrator is fully reliable;
- whether the narrator has ties to the people hunting Pauli.

The narrator may begin to be revealed around Episodes 2–3, but the full relationship to Pauli should remain unresolved longer.

### Narrative behavior
The narrator should:
- know slightly more than expected;
- occasionally contradict the official surveillance feed;
- sometimes omit information deliberately;
- make small verbal slips that become meaningful later;
- speak from memory rather than exposition;
- allow other witnesses to disagree.

The audience should eventually have two simultaneous questions:
1. Where is Pauli?
2. Who is telling us this story?

## Pauli mystery rule
Pauli should function as a legend before he functions as a fully visible character.

For roughly the first 7–8 episodes:
- no clean verified face reveal;
- no conventional hero introduction;
- no complete explanation of his mission;
- use consequences, objects, witness stories, wanted posters, locations, vehicles, rumors, reflections, conflicting records, and indirect sightings.

The audience should construct an idea of Pauli before the series begins proving or disproving those assumptions.

## Faceless cast rule
The Yappyverse is predominantly faceless.

Characters may be shown through:
- backs;
- silhouettes;
- reflections;
- shadows;
- low umbrellas;
- masks;
- partial bodies;
- transformed/shapeshifted forms;
- surveillance errors;
- obstructed sightlines.

A clean face reveal is a rare story event and may be tied to screenshot, sighting, prize, or other game mechanics.

## Protective-cast rule
Supporting Yappyverse characters are generally protective of Pauli, but the audience should not understand why at first.

Their actions may initially look suspicious, evasive, or criminal when viewed through hostile surveillance. Later episodes may reframe those same actions as protection, rescue, misdirection, or mission support.

## Shapeshifter rule
Yappyverse characters are future-origin animal beings capable of changing apparent form.

Different observers or sensors may perceive different forms. Conflicting witness descriptions do not necessarily mean somebody is lying.

This permits:
- contradictory wanted posters;
- incompatible biometric readings;
- human/animal/unknown classifications from surveillance;
- rare human-facing manifestations;
- later revelations that multiple earlier descriptions were simultaneously valid.

Do not explain this system early. Let the audience encounter contradictions first.

## Information-release rule
Evidence appears before meaning.

Examples:
- food banks, shelters, youth programs, and other social-purpose locations may appear as ordinary background or surveillance stops long before the audience understands why Pauli is repeatedly connected to them;
- the Cadillac may first appear to be a tracking vulnerability before being reinterpreted as deliberate misdirection;
- low Seattle umbrellas may initially look like weather behavior before being revealed as anti-drone counter-surveillance;
- official drone labels may later be revealed as biased, incomplete, or hostile framing.

Do not explain Pauli's social-purpose mission early.

## Narration restraint for Episode 001
Use only a few short narrator interventions.

Preferred lines currently locked for development:

> Nobody could tell you where Pauli was. But everybody knew where he'd been.

At the vehicle reveal:

> And everybody knew the car.

These lines establish reputation and evidence without explaining Pauli's mission.

## Story authority rule
No narrator, witness, drone, wanted poster, database, news report, or character has complete authority.

Every source may contain:
- incomplete knowledge;
- perspective;
- agenda;
- mistaken assumptions;
- deliberately withheld information.

The audience assembles the truth.

## Creative influence boundary
Use general mystery and crime-story principles such as unreliable narration, myth-building through indirect evidence, conflicting testimony, delayed identity, reversals, and recontextualization.

Do not copy dialogue, plot, characters, scenes, prose, or distinctive expression from existing films, books, or television.

## Production consequence
All episode maps should now track:
- what the narrator says;
- what the narrator knows;
- what the narrator withholds;
- what the audience assumes;
- what surveillance claims;
- what is actually true in canon;
- what later episode will reinterpret the clue.
